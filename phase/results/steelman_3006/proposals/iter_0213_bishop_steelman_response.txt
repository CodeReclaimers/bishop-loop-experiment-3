CRITIQUE: The suggestion to use a regex for skipping whitespace is problematic because regex engines, even pre-compiled ones, are not faster than tight C-level loops for simple character-class matching. Additionally, regex matching introduces overhead from state management and backtracking that can slow down the common case of minimal whitespace. A more serious issue is that regex-based approaches can break on very large inputs due to stack exhaustion or memory pressure from backtracking.

STEELMAN: The steelman version would use a compiled regex to efficiently skip whitespace by matching one or more whitespace characters in a single operation, but only when there's a significant amount of whitespace to skip — essentially combining the efficiency of regex for bulk skipping with the performance of direct iteration for small amounts.
