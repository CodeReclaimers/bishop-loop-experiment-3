CRITIQUE: The suggestion to use `text.find(''.join(' \t\n\r'), pos)` is flawed because `find()` returns the index of the *first* occurrence of any whitespace character, not the index of the *first non-whitespace character*. This would incorrectly advance the position past whitespace instead of stopping at the first non-whitespace character. Additionally, `find()` is not necessarily faster than a simple loop for small amounts of whitespace, and could be slower due to the overhead of constructing the search string.

STEELMAN: A better approach is to use `re.search()` with a precompiled regex that matches one or more whitespace characters, then advance the position by the length of the match. This preserves the intent of leveraging fast built-in string operations while correctly identifying the position of the first non-whitespace character.
