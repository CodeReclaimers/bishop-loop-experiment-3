CRITIQUE: The suggestion to reduce `_skip_ws` calls is problematic because it would break the parsing logic in `_parse_object` and `_parse_array` where whitespace needs to be skipped *between* structural elements (like after a key, after a colon, after a comma, etc.), not just once per token. Simply skipping whitespace after each token would skip over required whitespace in the middle of parsing, causing incorrect behavior or missed tokens. Additionally, this approach fails to account for the fact that `_skip_ws` is used in multiple contexts with different expectations about when whitespace should be consumed.

STEELMAN: The steelman is to strategically reduce redundant `_skip_ws` calls by consolidating them *only* where it's safe to do so — i.e., after reading a complete structural token (like a key, value, or comma) and before proceeding to the next logical parsing step. This preserves correctness while minimizing function call overhead, but must still be applied carefully to avoid skipping whitespace in the wrong places.
